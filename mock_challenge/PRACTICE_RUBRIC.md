# DeepData timed-mock self-scorecard (100 points)

This is an **invented training rubric**. It is NOT the organizers' official scoring criteria; adapt to the actual rules revealed on October 23.

| Category | Points | What excellent looks like |
|---|---:|---|
| Problem framing & decision relevance | 15 | Clear objective, stakeholders, outcome, applicable decision |
| Data quality and leakage prevention | 20 | Missingness/duplicates/types checked, prediction-time availability checked, appropriate splitting |
| Model or analytic validity | 20 | Suitable metric, reasonable baseline, untouched holdout, realistic limitations |
| Insights & visualization | 15 | Accurate charts with denominators/units and evidence-backed recommendations |
| Usability & execution | 15 | Working reproducible notebook/dashboard, easy to operate, no broken demo |
| Communication & teamwork | 15 | Three-minute story, clean transitions, clear Q&A, shared understanding |
| **Total** | **100** | |

## Performance bands

- **90–100:** Strong ready-to-compete practice submission.
- **75–89:** Solid; repair biggest evidence/validation weakness.
- **60–74:** Functioning proof of concept; insufficient rigor or communication.
- **Below 60:** Simplify to reliable baseline and defendable insight.

## Two-person peer audit (required)

- Auditor 1 attempts installation and runs the app without developer help.
- Auditor 2 searches for hidden leakage, unjustified claims, denominator errors and hard-coded synthetic outputs.
- Both must identify one limitation that the speaker should say aloud.

## Mock retrospective

Write down: what broke, how long was wasted, whether results were reproducible, one weakness for each team role, and exactly one fix to implement before the next mock.
